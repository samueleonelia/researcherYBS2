# Primaries; Mail votes; Immigration; Iran; China; Jones Act; Inflation; MAGA Media | Yaron Brook Show

## Topics

- Democratic primaries and midterm outlook — large — He reads the primaries as showing the Democratic Party lurching hard left (DSA-aligned candidates), while a rare moderate win in Wisconsin and Mike Lindell's loss in Minnesota show the shift isn't a one-way track; he expects a modest Democratic "blue wave" in November not because voters like Democrats but because they hate Trump and Republicans even more, with no party actually popular with the country.
- Trump's mail-in ballot executive order — small — He sides with the courts that blocked Trump's attempt at federal control over mail voting, calling it an unconstitutional power grab since election administration belongs to states and Congress, and doubts the Supreme Court will touch it before the midterms.
- Immigration policy, especially high-skilled/legal immigration — large — He argues Trump's proposed $100,000 fee for foreign graduates to stay and work proves MAGA's anti-immigration stance was never just about illegal immigration but extends to legal, high-skilled immigrants too, and cites research showing skilled immigrants expand the job pool and raise native wages rather than competing for a fixed number of jobs.
- Iran sanctions/blockade strategy — medium — He argues Trump's bet that economic pressure alone will force Iran to capitulate is a losing strategy because the regime is willing to let its population suffer indefinitely and has no internal force capable of overthrowing it, so only sustained military action toward regime change can actually end the conflict.
- China trade policy and industrial planning — medium — He argues Trump's tariffs have failed to reduce dependence on China or revive US manufacturing, so the administration is now resorting to subsidies, equity stakes, and import bans (e.g. robotics, solar components) that amount to left-style central planning and will only raise prices further.
- Jones Act waiver — small — He praises the temporary waiver (allowing foreign vessels to ship between US ports) for cutting prices and boosting domestic trade, but criticizes Trump's narrower 90-day renewal requiring voyage-by-voyage bureaucratic approval as needless, special-interest-driven inefficiency; he wants the Jones Act repealed outright.
- Inflation/CPI data — small — He treats the July CPI figures as still a big miss against the Fed's 2% target, argues people react to relative price pain (groceries, gas) regardless of its cause, and notes chicken prices have fallen sharply even as tariffs and oil push other prices up.
- MAGA media influencer decline — medium — He treats the sharp drop in views for most top pro-Trump influencers (except Tucker Carlson) as real evidence MAGA's media dominance is fracturing, while noting with some frustration that this decline hasn't translated into growth for his own show.
- American vs. European living standards — small — He uses net-disposable-income comparisons and rising US passport ownership to argue Americans remain far richer than Europeans even after accounting for "free" healthcare, and that European economic policy is somehow even worse than Trump's.
- Fort Knox gold — small — He notes Rand Paul's visit confirmed the gold reserves are real and argues the government should simply sell the gold to pay down debt rather than hold it for a hypothetical return to a gold standard.
- Audience Q&A (social media/tobacco liability, Faulkner, robotaxis, interior design, favorite food cities, executive orders, Gaza casualty figures, AI skepticism) — large (combined) — He takes personal-responsibility positions throughout: no corporate liability for addictive-but-non-forcible products, skepticism of high Gaza civilian casualty claims (citing Andrew Fox's data), general enthusiasm for AI and self-driving cars, and skepticism of most executive-order authority beyond FDR-era norms.

## Running threads

- Trump/MAGA as self-destructive for America — extended to immigration (the $100k grad fee), China policy (tariffs failing, now central planning), and the Jones Act bureaucracy, reinforcing his standing claim that Trump is an "American destroyer" economically.
- Iran regime-change argument — repeats his standing view that only sustained military pressure, not sanctions/blockade, can end the war, and that Trump has effectively abandoned that option.
- Gaza casualty skepticism — repeats his reliance on Andrew Fox's substack data and the claim that Gaza's population has grown since October 7, used again to dismiss high civilian death tolls as inflated/anti-Israel bias.
- MAGA media audience decline — a continuing data point he tracks (his own show's flat growth despite MAGA's decline is a recurring self-deprecating aside).
- American economic superiority over Europe — recurring comparison (net disposable income, passport ownership) he returns to periodically to rebut "free healthcare" arguments.

## Moves

- Distinguishes support for a politician's personal traits (business success, intelligence) from support for their political ideas and character — refuses to endorse "productive people" in politics just because they're productive (used against Mike Lindell and implicitly Trump).
- Treats sustained, multi-year data trends (passport growth, income gaps, viewer numbers) as more reliable than single data points or narratives pushed to fit a political story.
- Applies a strict personal-responsibility/agency standard to liability questions: companies or products are only blameworthy when they use force or fraud, not when people "choose" to overuse or become addicted.
- Frames government economic intervention as a self-perpetuating cycle: failed interventions (tariffs) generate calls for more intervention (subsidies, bans) rather than a return to free markets.
- Uses cost/benefit and incentive analysis to unmask "public interest" rhetoric as cover for narrow special-interest lobbying (Jones Act boat owners, tariff beneficiaries).
