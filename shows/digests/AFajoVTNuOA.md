# Mamdani's Supermarket; El-Sayed; FTC; Robots; Gaza; Hormuz; Profits; Achievement | Yaron Brook Show

## Topics

- Mamdani's city-run supermarkets — largest segment — He calls the plan a disaster in waiting: the promised 30% discount can't come from efficiency or squeezed profit margins (grocery margins run about 2%), so it has to be a taxpayer subsidy, meaning a wealth transfer from taxpayers to whoever happens to shop there (not means-tested, and not concentrated among the poor), with shortages and rationing inevitable given only five stores against roughly 1,000+ grocery stores and thousands of bodegas citywide; he predicts the plan collapses or stalls after one or two stores.
- Abdul El-Sayed wins Michigan Senate Democratic primary — large segment — He argues El-Sayed, a Medicare-for-all progressive and associate of a communist figure he calls "Pah," is too far left and too anti-Israel to win Michigan's general election, so his nomination likely costs Democrats the Senate seat, which he sees as a good outcome; he attributes the primary win to young/college and Arab-American voters and to El-Sayed being more ideologically principled than his rival, which he thinks is exactly why hard-left candidates keep winning primaries.
- FTC's proposed AI-bias regulation — large segment — He calls this a clear First Amendment violation and the most dangerous story of the show: the government positioning itself as arbiter of "truth" and "bias" in AI outputs is censorship regardless of party, disproving the notion that Trump/Republicans are pro-free-speech.
- Robot/drone import ban on Chinese tech — medium segment — He argues the ban (covering nearly all networked ground robots, including future Roombas) is either bogus national-security theater or protectionism for companies like Tesla, and either way it reduces Americans' standard of living and quality of life while inviting Chinese retaliation (e.g., restricting drone exports).
- Gaza ceasefire/disarmament deal reversal — medium segment — He argues Trump's new deal, which has Israel withdraw before Hamas disarms (reversing the original sequence), is a capitulation that Netanyahu has rightly rejected, and reflects a Turkey/Qatar/Egypt-brokered arrangement hostile to Israel's interests.
- Strait of Hormuz deal between Iran and Oman — medium segment — He argues the emerging arrangement, letting Iran and Oman split control and toll revenue over shipping through the strait, is a US capitulation that hands Iran a maritime-law-defying victory it didn't have before the war, and he is skeptical of Pentagon claims that the strait is open or that US missile stockpiles are fine.
- Trump attacking oil company "windfall profits" — short segment — He mocks Trump for adopting Democrats' windfall-profits rhetoric against Exxon and Chevron, taking it as further proof Trump is not genuinely pro-market or pro-capitalism.
- Positive tech stories: Joby Aviation/Atmos flying-taxi infrastructure and AST SpaceMobile's Bluebird satellites — short segment — He's enthusiastic about both as genuine progress, praising Travis Kalanick's involvement in Atmos and the potential for direct-to-cell satellites to make ground cellular infrastructure (or even Starlink) less necessary.
- Academic paper on cultural values and economic growth (Suffansky's AI text-mining study) — short segment — He endorses the paper's finding that declining social hierarchy and fear of the unknown, alongside rising individual autonomy and patience across Western texts from 1000-1920, tracks with and likely drove the rise of capitalism and productivity growth, calling it a valuable empirical validation of ideas-drive-history claims.
- Q&A: Christian nationalism vs. Islam in America, stakeholder theory, Ceuta migrant crisis — short segment — He rejects "Christian America" claims (America was founded secular, on individual rights, not Christianity) and says Christians pose a bigger institutional threat to liberty than the small Muslim population; on Ceuta he blames Morocco for engineering the migrant surge and argues Europe's immigration crisis stems from its welfare state, not the immigrants themselves; on stakeholder theory he calls it an unworkable, directionless standard compared to shareholder wealth maximization.

## Running threads

- Iran/Strait of Hormuz negotiations — continues his running skepticism of Trump's Iran diplomacy, this time flagging a reported Iran-Oman deal that would formalize Iranian control over shipping tolls, and repeating his view that only regime toppling ends the standoff.
- US missile stockpile shortage — he again raises the claim (denied by the Pentagon) that the US is running low on Tomahawks, ATACMS, and Patriot/THAAD interceptors, a point he's made before.
- Trump not actually pro-free-speech / not actually pro-market — recurring theme, applied here to the FTC AI ruling and to Trump's windfall-profits attack on oil companies.
- Israel-Hamas ceasefire terms — continues tracking the disarm-before/after-withdrawal sequencing dispute, noting Netanyahu's rejection of Trump's reordered version.

## Moves

- Follows the subsidy money: whenever a program promises a below-market price, he asks where the missing margin actually comes from and treats "the taxpayer" as the answer.
- Uses base-rate comparison to puncture symbolic gestures (e.g., five city stores vs. thousands of existing grocery stores/bodegas) to show a policy can't do what it claims.
- Applies a consistent standard against selective outrage: judges "free speech" and "pro-market" claims by actions, not party labels, to show Republicans fail their own professed principles as often as Democrats.
- Treats ideas/culture as the causal driver of economic outcomes, citing empirical studies to back the objectivist claim that philosophy (values around hierarchy, autonomy, risk-tolerance) shapes history rather than merely reflecting it.
- Frames immigration and welfare-state problems as a rights/rule-of-law failure rather than a demographic or religious one.
