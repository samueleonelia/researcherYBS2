# Tariffs; Meta; ABC; Russia; Economy; China AI; Publishing; Antitrust; Achievements | Yaron Brook Show

## Topics

- Trump's tariffs on Canada — largest segment — He condemns the 50% tariffs (imposed via a never-before-used Smoot-Hawley provision) as pure self-harm that raises Americans' cost of living, and argues Canada should unilaterally go to zero tariffs and ignore the US rather than negotiate.
- US debt, deficits and bond yields — large segment — He argues the debt crisis (nearing $40 trillion, rising interest costs) is now visibly showing up in surging long-term bond yields across the US, Japan and UK, driven by fear of default/inflation, and that only spending cuts (including entitlement reform) can fix it, which nobody in Washington is willing to do.
- Meta/Facebook lawsuits over youth mental health — medium segment — He thinks the addictive design claims are real but rejects the idea Meta should be legally liable, insisting parents, not platforms, are responsible for children's device and app use.
- ABC suing the FCC — medium segment — He strongly supports ABC's First Amendment lawsuit against FCC license reviews he sees as Trump-driven retaliation for critical coverage (including the Kimmel matter), and hopes it goes all the way to the Supreme Court in ABC's favor.
- Russia repression and economic strain — medium segment — He cites the 11-year sentence for an antiwar opposition politician and the firing of a state-company economist who warned of a war-of-attrition economic crisis as proof Russia is an authoritarian, deteriorating state with zero tolerance for dissent.
- Positive news roundup (crime decline, immigration/crime study, Argentina shale, African payments network, NYC rat sightings) — medium segment — He presents falling US violent crime, no crime link to illegal immigration, Argentina's shale boom, African cross-border payment integration, and NYC's rat decline as underreported good news showing markets/reform working, while crediting Biden-era trends (not Trump) for the crime drop.
- China's AI training data — small segment — He warns that China is pushing its own censored/biased data into global AI training sets, and that any model trained on biased data will produce biased results since AI cannot independently evaluate truth.
- Publishing industry and AI-written books — small segment — He says publishers have no clear, workable standard for what counts as improper AI use in authorship, so they're pulling books reactively amid unreliable AI-detection tools.
- Antitrust probe of Andreessen Horowitz over interlocking boards — small segment — He calls it a minor, oddly-timed case under an old 1914 law, noting Trump's DOJ is essentially continuing the Biden DOJ's approach despite Andreessen and Horowitz being Trump donors.
- Index vs. active investing — small aside — He reiterates his standing advice that passive index investing beats active stock-picking, citing a new study showing only ~13% of active large-cap funds beat their benchmark over a decade.

## Running threads

- US debt/deficit spiral — continues a recurring theme, now tied concretely to rising 30-year bond yields (5.3%, highest since 2007) as evidence markets are pricing in default/inflation risk.
- Falling US crime rate — he reiterates a claim he's made since 2022 despite past pushback, now bolstered by FBI data showing the largest annual drop on record, crediting the trend to before Trump/ICE.
- Russia's war economy deteriorating — continuing thread on Russian economic and political fragility amid the Ukraine war, now with an insider (fired state economist) publicly warning of crisis.
- Trump and tariffs as economically destructive — continuing critique of Trump's trade policy as a tax that raises costs and fuels cronyism, tied back to America's first 1789/1796 tariff history.

## Moves

- Free-market/free-trade principle: unilateral free trade benefits the country that adopts it regardless of what trading partners do.
- Individual responsibility standard: harms attributed to companies (Meta, addictive apps) are reframed as failures of personal/parental responsibility, not corporate liability.
- Government-versus-market framing: attributes economic and social problems (inflation, cost of living, crime narratives) to statism and government policy rather than to capitalism.
- Skepticism of government claims: urges treating "national security" and similar official justifications as requiring independent verification because officials may be lying or lack a real strategy.
- First Amendment / rule-of-law framing: judges government actions against media and speech by whether they constitute retaliation against protected speech, independent of policy outcome.
