# Tucker Running?; Iran; Griftathon; Yen; Inflation; Cuba; Ceuta; mRNA | Yaron Brook Show

## Topics

- Tucker Carlson's political ambitions and 10-point "manifesto" — roughly half the show — He argues Tucker is quietly building a third-party/movement (uniting far-left and MAGA-right) around hatred of Israel and hatred of capitalism/finance, and that Tucker mixes genuinely appealing points (anti-corruption, honesty, government transparency, beauty in architecture, health) with authoritarian, collectivist, anti-Semitic, and anti-modern content (debt-as-slavery/anti-usury rhetoric, hostility to trade and technology, glorification of farming/manual labor, anti-immigrant unity-through-sameness, dishonest claims about fertility rates); he concludes Tucker is smart, not a nutcase to most voters, and would be far more dangerous than Trump if he ran.
- Iran / Strait of Hormuz deal and US munitions shortage — moderate — He argues the reported Oman-brokered deal to reopen the Strait effectively hands Iran de facto control over Gulf shipping traffic and would be a major strategic loss if Trump signs off, since Gulf states may just accept Iranian-approved routes; separately he says the US munitions shortage (Patriot missiles, Tomahawks) is real, and that Trump's denial and threats to prosecute leakers for "treason" is dishonest spin rather than an actual rebuttal.
- Trump's Truth Social pre-release trading scheme ("Griftathon") — small — He calls the $100,000/month sale of advance access to Trump's market-moving posts blatant corruption and effectively insider trading, serious enough on its own to justify impeachment, and notes it follows on the heels of the withdrawn $1.8B "anti-weaponization fund" scandal, which Trump now wants to codify into law.
- Japanese yen collapse and US bailout — moderate — He explains the yen is falling despite Bank of Japan rate hikes because of Japan's unsustainable debt, and argues the US is now printing dollars to buy yen so Japan won't dump US Treasuries to fund its own yen support, which he sees as a worrying sign of America's own fiscal fragility (rising 30-year yields, $2T annual deficits) being papered over with money-printing.
- Inflation measurement changes (PCE index) — small — He argues that a Commerce Department recalibration that will lower reported inflation by about 0.2% is not Trump manipulation but routine statistical adjustment, and reiterates his long-held view that official price-inflation measures generally overstate true inflation because they fail to capture quality improvements.
- Cuba's power/economic crisis and partial privatization — moderate — He argues Cuba's blackouts and food shortages are the direct result of decades of communism plus the US embargo/maximum-pressure campaign, but sees the regime's incremental privatization of fuel importing and distribution as a small, hopeful sign of liberalization, while remaining skeptical the regime will fully embrace reform or collapse soon.
- Ceuta migrant crossing (Spanish enclave in Morocco) — moderate — He revises his earlier theory that Morocco orchestrated the crossing to pressure Spain, concluding instead it was driven by a false social-media rumor about automatic EU citizenship, that guards were simply overwhelmed, that roughly 100 people died in a real tragedy, that everyone was expelled within 48 hours with no actual "invasion" of Europe, and that Spain's internal politics (Catalonia legalizing Moroccan/Arab immigrants to avoid Spanish-speakers, Madrid legalizing Latin Americans) shape which immigrants get status.
- FDA approval of an mRNA flu vaccine (mNexviva/Moderna) for adults 50+ — small — He treats this as a surprising, positive development given HHS's anti-mRNA posture, noting trial data showing meaningfully better efficacy than standard flu vaccines.

## Running threads

- Tucker Carlson as a future presidential threat — he has predicted for years Tucker would eventually run, and now treats this week's meetings and 10-point speech as movement-building whether or not he formally runs.
- Left-right authoritarian unity — a thesis he says he's held for over a decade, previously expecting it to form around environmentalism; he now says it's forming around shared hatred of Israel and hatred of capitalism instead.

- Cuba's slow-motion liberalization — he keeps noting incremental reform announcements (since a July 30th package) as a running tally, expecting continued small steps rather than sudden regime collapse.
- US government corruption under Trump — treats the Truth Social trading scheme as the latest entry in a continuing pattern (following the anti-weaponization fund episode) of brazen, undeniable corruption.

## Moves

- He judges political rhetoric by whether it's grounded in explicit principles (especially individual rights) versus vague appeals to "fairness," "unity," or "sovereignty" that a smart operator can fill with authoritarian content.
- He treats morality and economics as inseparable: an economic policy or figure's popularity reflects an underlying moral premise (self-interest vs. altruism/sacrifice), and he traces support for socialism or protectionism back to unrefuted moral premises rather than mere ignorance.
- He repeatedly flags rhetorical sleight-of-hand — mixing a few true, appealing claims with false or authoritarian ones — as the core danger of skilled demagogues like Tucker and Trump.
- He evaluates claims by comparing them against verifiable global data (e.g., actual fertility-rate rankings) to expose factual distortion.
- He frames immigration, trade, and technology debates around productivity/GDP growth and voluntary exchange, opposing autarky, protectionism, and appeals to "real" (manual) labor as the only legitimate production.
