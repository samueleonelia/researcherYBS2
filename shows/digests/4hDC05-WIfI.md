# War; Missiles; Tariffs; DEA; Fed; AI; Cockroach; Millionaires; Lithium; Milei | Yaron Brook Show

## Topics

- Iran war and Trump's handling of it — large, opening segment — Trump has no strategy at all, his pauses and threats to "hit them hard" are empty, and his unwillingness to finish the war has only emboldened Iran and let it project strength it hasn't earned; Iran is winning by simply waiting Trump out while oil prices climb.
- US munitions shortage vs. defense-tech startups — large — the Pentagon's old-line procurement system (Patriot, THAAD, Tomahawk production) is slow, politically fragmented, and dangerously depleted, but private venture-funded startups (Anduril, Mach Industries, Saronic, Singularity) are proving that free-market capitalism, not top-down five-year planning, can rebuild American defense manufacturing fast if the Pentagon gets out of the way.
- Trump's tariffs — medium-large — the new Section 301 "forced labor" tariffs replacing the reciprocal tariffs the Supreme Court struck down are just as arbitrary and legally dubious as the originals, expanding to Canada, Brazil, and dozens more countries, and are not achieving their stated goal (e.g. aluminum tariffs aren't bringing smelters back, just raising costs and pushing suppliers to sell elsewhere).
- DEA report on drug-boat strikes — small-medium — a DEA analysis confirms Trump's illegal strikes on suspected drug boats (221+ killed) have not reduced cocaine entering the US, exactly as Yaron predicted, because traffickers simply shifted routes ("squeeze the balloon").
- Inflation and the Fed — small-medium — current price increases are mostly a supply shock (oil, tariffs) not true monetary inflation, so raising interest rates is the wrong tool; the Fed should instead shrink the money supply/balance sheet, and he's glad the Fed held rates and defied Trump's pressure to cut.
- AI slowdown petition — small — the petition by frontier-lab employees asking government to slow AI development is self-serving regulatory capture by "leftist crybabies" trying to lock in their lead and get government protection, not a genuine safety measure.
- India's "cockroach protests" — medium — young, educated, unemployed Indians turned a Chief Justice's insult ("cockroaches and parasites") into a viral protest movement over leaked exams, forced Modi's education minister to resign, and shows that even a seemingly untouchable government must bend when it turns against its own base — though the government is now quietly reneging via the courts.
- UK vs US millionaire counts — small — the UK's collapsing millionaire population (down from over 1 million to 442,000) shows the damage of high taxes and socialism, while the US adding 1,200 millionaires a day shows genuine wealth creation and economic confidence.
- Lithium-ion battery price collapse — small — a roughly 99% price drop since 1991 is a triumph of markets: innovation, competition, and scale, not government, driving down costs.
- Argentina/Milei — small — Argentina's third credit-rating upgrade in three months, falling inflation, and a fiscal surplus validate Milei's free-market reforms as a genuine model for other stagnant economies, aided further by huge untapped oil/gas potential from privatized fracking.
- Q&A topics (West Bank/settlers, Marxist vs. Randian "triangle," German 1930s nihilism, Israel's stature after the war, small talk and individualism, envy of billionaires, vaccine liability) — remainder of show — recurring positions: West Bank violence is the fault of lawless religious nationalist settlers and a government that won't prosecute them; Rand's inverted pyramid shows value flows from capital allocators down, refuting Marx; American friendliness reflects individualism/trader mentality vs. collectivist zero-sum resentment; envy of the wealthy is both an emotional and a rationalized moral (egalitarian) position; he's skeptical of anti-vaccine claims and defers to the scientific consensus.

## Running threads

- Iran war/no-strategy critique — continues from earlier shows; adds the DEA-style empirical pattern (pauses, threats, then de-facto capitulation) and the observation that Trump's inaction has itself elevated Iran's global stature.
- Munitions shortage — a recurring number/theme; this time he gives detailed depletion stats (Patriot ~65% down, THAAD ~38% down) and contrasts them with startup progress (Anduril's Arsenal-1 factory, Saronic's autonomous ships).
- Tariffs as unconstitutional/irrational policy — an ongoing thread since "Liberation Day"; adds the Section 301 replacement tariffs, new Canada dispute, and the aluminum-tariff failure as fresh evidence.
- Milei/Argentina as a positive economic model — recurring good-news segment; adds the third rating upgrade and new detail on Argentina's fracking/oil potential.

## Moves

- Judge policies/wars by whether their factual claims are true, not by the rhetorical parallel between two sides' claims (used against equating US-Iran and Russia-Ukraine).
- Distinguish price increases (supply shocks) from true inflation (a monetary/demand phenomenon), and judge Fed policy by that distinction.
- Favor private capital and entrepreneurial competition over centralized government planning as the mechanism that actually solves hard problems (defense manufacturing, battery costs).
- Treat egalitarian/anti-wealth arguments as a mix of genuine envy and a rationalizing moral doctrine (altruism/egalitarianism), and diagnose "concern for the public interest" as being used inconsistently by people who reject it elsewhere.
- Read government climb-downs (Modi, past farm law repeal) as evidence that a movement succeeds when it turns the regime's own base against it, not merely the opposition.
- Apply an individualism-vs-collectivism lens to social behavior (friendliness, resentment, envy) as a recurring explanatory framework.
