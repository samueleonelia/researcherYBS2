# Bonds; Gerontocracy; Canada; Iran; Ukraine; DSA; Nvidia; Achievement | Yaron Brook Show

## Topics

- The bond market and Treasury Secretary Bessent's yield-curve manipulation — largest segment — Long-term interest rates have risen sharply because investors rightly doubt the government will ever repay $40+ trillion in debt, and Bessent's bond-buyback scheme (even scaled to a rumored $1 trillion) is powerless central planning that shifts money between pockets without touching the underlying fundamentals; the only real fix is cutting spending, which no one in government will do.
- Entitlements and the "gerontocracy" transferring wealth from young to old — large segment — Social Security and Medicare/Medicaid dominate and keep growing federal spending, cannot be fixed by taxing the rich or even the middle class since the sums needed are too vast, and persist because old people vote, dominate activist politics, and refuse to have the programs touched, even though he argues old people themselves would be better off under a smaller, freer, faster-growing economy.
- Trump's tariff war with Canada and China — large segment — Canada was right to walk away from negotiations rather than accept US demands, but should have unilaterally dropped its own tariffs to zero instead of retaliating; Trump's new 50% tariffs on Canadian autos/materials and added China tariffs raise costs on homebuilders and US automakers and are "economic stupidity," contradicting his stated goal of lowering the cost of living, while South American trade blocs are doing the opposite by expanding free trade.
- New US sanctions on Iran ("Operation Economic Outcast") — medium segment — Iran's economy is collapsing (currency crash, falling GDP, food inflation) but the regime, controlled by the IRGC, won't capitulate just because its people suffer, and he's skeptical the new sanctions campaign will succeed given 47 years of sanctions have accomplished little, especially since Bessent pointedly avoided naming China, the one country whose cooperation would actually matter.
- Nvidia's vertically-integrated investment strategy across the AI industry — medium segment — Nvidia is now investing in nearly every AI company and data-center venture that buys its chips (OpenAI, Anthropic, Perplexity, Mistral, xAI, and more), which he finds a clever but concerning business strategy that would draw serious antitrust scrutiny if Nvidia weren't a politically favored industry, and that raises real risk of a bubble if the sky-high valuations underlying these circular investments don't hold up.
- The Democratic Socialists of America's platform — small-medium segment — DSA is more honest than mainstream Democrats about wanting to abolish capitalism, the Senate, and aid to Israel, but its vision is closer to East Germany than Scandinavia, and he laments that neither DSA's left nor JD Vance's statist new right leaves anyone actually defending capitalism.
- Ukraine's weapons production and UK missile-tech sharing — small segment — Britain letting Ukraine build Storm Shadow cruise missile components domestically is strategically smarter than just sending finished weapons because it builds Ukraine's own military-industrial capacity, a policy the US should copy with Patriot production instead of protecting Raytheon/Lockheed from competition.
- Achievement segment: humanoid robots and NYC deregulation — small segment — He's impressed that a Chinese humanoid robot now runs the 100m faster than Usain Bolt, showing rapid robotics progress, and grudgingly praises NYC's modest package of business deregulation (even under a socialist mayor) as proof that deregulation, not redistribution, is what actually helps small business and housing supply.

## Running threads

- US debt/deficit trajectory — continues citing the ~$40 trillion national debt, projects it to $60-94 trillion within a decade, and reiterates that rising interest costs (now over $1 trillion/year) make any crisis response unaffordable.
- Critique of Bessent as Treasury Secretary — builds on earlier skepticism, now arguing directly that Bessent, despite hype as "the adult in the room," is a failed hedge-fund manager acting like a central planner who thinks he knows better than the market.
- Trump's tariff policy as self-defeating — continues his repeated argument that tariffs raise costs for Americans and that Trump doubles down regardless of evidence, this time applied fresh to Canada and China.
- Nobody defends capitalism — repeats his recurring lament that both the online left and right (DSA and JD Vance's national conservatism) are statist/Marxist-influenced, with literally no one making the moral or economic case for markets.
- Personal note on Israel — continues an ongoing thread (referencing "yesterday's" comments) about his ambivalence toward Israel, saying he enjoys short visits but finds something collectivistic and oppressive about it and doesn't share others' nostalgia for home, tying it to his mother's recent death and his father's declining health.

## Moves

- Distinguishes "reality-based" market pricing from government "financial trickery"/manipulation, judging policy by whether it lets prices reflect real information or tries to override it.
- Uses a compare-to-history standard (comparing today's rates, spending, or deficits to the 1970s-1990s baseline) to judge whether something is truly abnormal or just a return to normal.
- Applies a "who actually pays / who benefits" distributional lens (e.g., tariffs paid by domestic consumers, entitlements paid by the young) to cut through political framing of policies.
- Judges policies by practical arithmetic before morality: shows a program is unaffordable on the numbers alone, separate from whether it's also immoral.
- Uses a consistency test against stated goals (e.g., Trump claims to want to lower cost of living while every action raises it) to expose contradiction as "economic stupidity" rather than strategy.
