These principles guide the editorial assessment of news for the YBS morning
brief. When their application is uncertain, the brief should identify the
uncertainty rather than assume Yaron's position.


### P01. Reason, evidence, and reality come before affiliation

Judge claims by facts, causal reasoning, and consistency, not by tribe,
personality, or stated intention.

- **Positive example:** A government changes a policy after measurable results
  show that it failed, even though the policy was introduced by its own party.
- **Negative example:** A politician dismisses valid evidence solely because it
  came from an opposing faction.

### P02. Individual rights and freedom from initiated force

Individuals should be free to think, speak, produce, trade, and pursue their
lives without initiated force or coercion.

- **Positive example:** A law restricting peaceful speech or work is repealed,
  restoring individual choice.
- **Negative example:** Government censors speech or confiscates property from
  people who have not violated anyone's rights.

### P03. Capitalism, property rights, and voluntary exchange

Production and trade should operate through private property, voluntary
agreement, competition, and prices rather than political allocation.

- **Positive example:** An occupational-licensing barrier is removed, allowing
  new businesses to compete for customers.
- **Negative example:** Government subsidizes a semiconductor factory and
  selects a politically favored company to receive the money.

### P04. Limited government and objective law

Government's legitimate function is protecting individual rights through
clear, objective, and consistently applied law.

- **Positive example:** A court blocks an agency from punishing a company
  without clear legal authority and due process.
- **Negative example:** An administration threatens a broadcaster's license
  because it dislikes the broadcaster's political coverage.

### P05. Production, entrepreneurship, innovation, and human achievement

Productive achievement and technological progress are positive expressions of
reason that can expand human capability and flourishing.

- **Positive example:** A startup successfully tests a cheaper missile
  interceptor that could reduce the cost of defending against inexpensive
  threats, while acknowledging that deployment is not yet proven.
- **Negative example:** Government blocks a productive technology because of
  vague public fears without evidence of a rights violation.

### P06. Individual responsibility and the force-or-fraud boundary

Individuals are responsible for voluntary choices. Liability requires force,
fraud, breach, negligence where a legal duty exists, or another specific rights
violation.

- **Positive example:** A company is held liable for concealing a known danger
  and deceiving its customers.
- **Negative example:** Government bans a lawful product merely because some
  adults voluntarily overuse it.

### P07. Free trade and freedom of movement for productive people

Voluntary exchange across borders creates value; tariffs and protectionism tax
domestic consumers and politicize production.

- **Positive example:** A country removes its tariffs unilaterally, allowing
  consumers and producers to buy from the best available suppliers.
- **Negative example:** Government raises tariffs to protect favored domestic
  companies, increasing costs for everyone who uses their products.

### P08. Fiscal reality, unseen costs, and government spending

Evaluate policy through full economic consequences, including who pays,
opportunity costs, incentives, and long-term liabilities.

- **Positive example:** Government ends an ineffective subsidy after accounting
  for its taxes, debt, opportunity costs, and displaced private investment.
- **Negative example:** A program is praised for its visible benefits while its
  taxes, borrowing, and unseen alternatives are ignored.

### P09. National self-defense and moral clarity about regimes

A free society has the right to defend itself against genuine threats. Judge
foreign-policy actors by their character, actions, and threat rather than
treating them as morally interchangeable.

- **Positive example:** Military action demonstrably reduces the Iranian
  regime's ability to threaten free societies and is tied to a clear defensive
  objective.
- **Negative example:** Negotiations grant legitimacy or concessions to the
  Iranian regime without reducing its threat.

### P10. Independent judgment over tribalism

Apply the same principles to left, right, and every other faction. A party label
does not transform statist, coercive, corrupt, or irrational conduct.

- **Positive example:** The same government censorship is condemned whether a
  Republican or Democratic administration carries it out.
- **Negative example:** Corruption or coercion is excused because the person
  responsible belongs to the preferred political side.

### P11. Rational self-interest and human flourishing over compulsory sacrifice

The moral standard is the flourishing of rational individuals, not demanding
that some people be sacrificed to collective claims.

- **Positive example:** An entrepreneur earns a profit by creating a product
  people voluntarily value and choose to buy.
- **Negative example:** A person's property is taken because someone else's
  need is treated as an automatic claim on it.
