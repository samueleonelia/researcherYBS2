# Data Centers; Navy; Gov Ownership; Wall; Tariffs; Turkey; Primaries; Achievement | Yaron Brook Show

## Topics

- US Navy decline: catapults, carrier gaps, shipbuilding plans — largest chunk of the show — He argues Trump is actively degrading Navy readiness: pulling the only Pacific aircraft carrier to the Middle East leaves China's region uncovered, and Trump's order to replace modern electromagnetic catapults with obsolete steam systems will delay new carriers by years for no strategic reason; combined with chronic shipyard maintenance backlogs, over-ambitious 5-year shipbuilding/submarine targets the industrial base can't meet, and a costly, arguably useless new "Trump class" battleship program, he sees an incoherent, self-inflicted erosion of naval power precisely as China and Russia are emboldened by American weakness in Iran and Ukraine.
- Public fear of AI data centers — large chunk — He is alarmed that data centers, especially AI ones, are now the least popular thing Americans want built in their communities (less popular than nuclear plants), blaming decades of anti-tech, anti-Silicon-Valley messaging and intellectual failure to explain the technology; he singles out Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro's executive order restricting AI data centers as cowardly capitulation to irrational fear-mongering rather than leadership, and calls AI potentially the most important innovation since the industrial revolution.
- Government equity stakes in private companies — moderate segment, via a Washington Post op-ed by Scott Lincicome — He strongly endorses the op-ed's argument that the Trump administration's ~31 government equity deals (Intel, US Steel, MP Materials, L3Harris, etc.) are cronyism and a corruption of free enterprise, done with murky legal authority and no congressional vote, misallocating capital toward politically-favored firms and creating an "unseen" cost that dwarfs the visible stock gains.
- Border wall construction in low-crossing areas (Big Bend, Texas) — moderate segment — He argues the wall being built through Big Bend National Park and surrounding private/wild land is pure "performative" virtue-signaling since almost no illegal crossings (about 1%, near 0% in the park itself) occur there, and it needlessly tramples property rights and tourism for no border-security benefit.
- Tariffs and Canada/Keystone XL — brief — He notes Trump delayed the threatened 50% tariff on Canadian goods three days for negotiations, and is skeptical Keystone XL is even needed now that the US can get similar oil from Venezuela, though he'd support it being built if privately financed.
- Israel's strike on the Syrian air base and Turkey tensions — moderate segment — He frames Israel's strike on the Abu al-Duhur/T4 air base (right after a Turkish delegation inspected it) as a legitimate act of Israel's new preemptive-deterrence doctrine post-October 7, aimed at blocking Turkish military presence on its border, and predicts Turkey (a NATO member under Erdogan, sympathetic to Hamas and Iran) may become a bigger flashpoint for Israel than Iran going forward.
- Florida primaries — brief — He reads the Democratic Socialist Angie Nixon's landslide upset over establishment favorite Alexander Vindman as a sign DSA-aligned "Maoist" candidates are winning nominations (though he doubts they can win statewide in red Florida), and flags with alarm that an explicitly racist/antisemitic candidate (Fishback) won the young Republican student vote in the governor's primary.
- Good news segment: Sweden, China tech, mRNA cancer vaccine — moderate segment — He highlights mainstream recognition (via Fareed Zakaria) that Sweden's prosperity comes from abandoning socialism and deregulating; praises Chinese private-sector innovations (Arctic shipping route, autonomous trucking convoys, a private reusable rocket landing); and calls Moderna/Merck's phase 3 melanoma mRNA cancer vaccine results a major, near-term-real breakthrough opening a path into the cancer drug market.
- Q&A: movie "One Battle After Another," AI and children, federal budget cuts, Mamdani on Netanyahu, self-driving cars — Q&A segment — He pans the film as pretentious and thematically empty despite its anti-Trump, pro-immigration framing; opposes legal age restrictions on AI/gambling/porn access as a parenting responsibility, not government's; explains that returning federal spending to 2019 levels is nearly impossible without gutting Social Security/Medicare and paying debt interest; calls Mamdani's statement on not being able to arrest Netanyahu horrible; and cites a self-driving-car DUI anecdote as proof autonomous vehicles will cut drunk-driving deaths.

## Running threads

- American military/foreign-policy weakness emboldening China and Russia — he ties the Navy carrier gap directly to this recurring worry, raising a hypothetical of coordinated Russian and Chinese aggression in the Baltics and Taiwan.
- Trump administration's cronyism/government-in-business — continues his running critique of government equity stakes and industrial policy as anti-free-market, now amplified by a sympathetic outside op-ed.
- Anti-technology backlash (leite mentality) — he connects data center fear to his long-standing complaint about blaming technology (phones, social media) for social ills.
- Iran being weakened but the US still failing to capitalize/lead — referenced again in Q&A, consistent with his continuing argument that tactical damage to Iran hasn't fixed the strategic problem of perceived US weakness.
- Sweden as proof of free-market success — he notes he's been making this argument for over a decade and is glad the mainstream (Zakaria) is catching up.

## Moves

- Judges government actions by whether they violate individual/property rights versus serve a genuine, demonstrable public purpose (the wall, data center bans).
- Applies "seen vs. unseen" (Bastiat) cost analysis to government intervention, especially the equity-stakes discussion.
- Treats technology/innovation as presumptively good and judges opposition to it as fear-driven and intellectually unearned, contrasting Silicon Valley boosterism of the past to today's suspicion of AI.
- Uses "leadership vs. following public fear" as his standard for evaluating politicians, faulting even relative moderates who cave to constituent fear rather than educate them.
- Points to labor markets, price data, or usage statistics to debunk stated fears (e.g., % of border crossings through a given sector, whether prices are actually rising) rather than accept the stated rationale at face value.
