# AI Manifesto; Cards; Munitions; Gaza; C/Attacks; Economy; AirTraffic; Achievement | Yaron Brook Show

## Topics

- Iran and the Strait of Hormuz standoff — largest single block of the show — Trump is reportedly ready to end the conflict by letting Iran keep control of the strait without a nuclear deal, which means Iran ends up with everything it had before the war plus proof the US won't act; he calls this a surrender and says Iran, not the US, now "has all the cards."
- Iran's nuclear and missile capability after the strikes — major, tied to the above — the bombing campaign destroyed facilities but never secured or located Iran's enriched uranium stockpile, so Iran is roughly 9-12 months from a bomb if it decides to build one, and he argues the war strengthened rather than ended Iran's strategic position.
- US munitions shortage / defense industry — significant — he says it's a scandal that the Pentagon, despite a trillion-dollar budget, let Patriot/interceptor/Tomahawk stocks run down through the Ukraine and Iran conflicts, and blames Lockheed and Raytheon for blocking Ukraine from building its own Patriots not over IP theft but because Ukraine could produce them cheaper and better, which he calls protectionism shielding incompetent contractors from competition.
- Zuckerberg's AI manifesto — significant — he praises it as the most positive, individualist vision to come out of a major AI CEO (framing AI as an amplifier of human ability rather than a replacer, rejecting centralized control), but faults Zuckerberg for treating freedom as a "balance of power" rather than individual rights, for pushing deep government-industry collaboration, and for denying there's an objective standard for a good life.
- Gaza ceasefire/disarmament stalemate — moderate — he backs Netanyahu's rejection of the Egypt/Qatar/Turkey-shaped 15-point plan that would have Israel withdraw before Hamas disarms, arguing Hamas will never actually disarm and that no Israeli government can afford to trust international guarantees after October 7th, though he thinks the current stalemate isn't bad for Israel since Hamas is currently not a threat.
- Cyberattacks on US water systems — brief — a multi-state cyberattack campaign (Iran suspected but unconfirmed) disrupted some utilities but caused no contaminated water, which he frames as a real resilience success once operators reverted to manual controls, though he says the exposure of control systems to the internet needs fixing.
- American wealth vs. economic pessimism — moderate — record numbers of Americans holding passports and traveling internationally, plus US household consumption leading the world even adjusted for cost of living, shows Americans are far richer than they perceive and than their gloomy sentiment about the economy suggests.
- Beef vs. chicken prices — brief — chicken producers overbuilt supply expecting demand to shift from expensive beef, prices collapsed and producer profits cratered, but consumers kept buying beef anyway, which he uses as an example of price/demand mismatch and of tariffs making beef artificially pricier.
- Air traffic controller hiring from gamers — brief — he approves of the FAA's campaign recruiting video gamers as controllers, calling it a legitimately fast, successful hiring push, while still arguing air traffic control should be privatized rather than run by government.
- Surveillance cameras and falling crime — brief — roughly 120,000 license-plate/flock cameras nationwide coincide with violent crime and auto theft down about 50%, which he credits partly to better identification/policing technology, while flagging privacy risks that could be managed through short data-retention limits.
- Domestic critical minerals (tungsten) — brief — a huge new US tungsten deposit found in Nevada is offered as proof that America isn't actually mineral-poor or dependent on China, just over-regulated on mining.

## Running threads

- Iran/Strait of Hormuz war — continues from prior shows (the "Putin has all the cards" line reused for Trump/Iran); this episode adds the reparations demand, the munitions-depletion angle, and Andrew Fox's substack analysis as new detail.
- Netanyahu/Israeli government criticism — recurring position that Netanyahu bears responsibility for October 7th and must go; repeats his view that Israel should focus solely on Iran and Hamas disarmament, not West Bank distractions.
- Zuckerberg/AI-industry commentary — continues his running critique of OpenAI/Anthropic-style "doom" and government-control framing in AI, contrasting Meta favorably this time.
- Inflation vs. wages debate with conservative commentators (Shapiro et al.) — recurring point that median income has outpaced inflation and this fact is being ignored on both sides of the political divide.

## Moves

- Distinguishes coercive government power from voluntary economic/market power as the core test of freedom, applied to AI regulation debates.
- Judges technology and companies by whether they expand individual capability and voluntary exchange, not by whether they preserve existing jobs or structures.
- Attributes strategic/military failures to incompetence and inability to think long-term/strategically, resisting explanations rooted in ideology (e.g., just war theory) as primary causes.
- Uses relative international comparisons (spending, consumption, wages vs. inflation) to challenge popular pessimism about the US economy.
- Insists that economic conclusions rest on individual rights and objective values, not moral relativism, and calls out arguments (like Zuckerberg's) that undercut their own premises by denying objective standards.
