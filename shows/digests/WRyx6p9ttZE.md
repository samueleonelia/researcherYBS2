# Labor Day; Afd; 9/11; Civilian Casualties; Gas Prices; VW; Iran; Achievements | Yaron Brook Show

## Topics

- Labor Day / capitalism and worker progress — largest segment — Life for workers has improved enormously since 1894 (higher real wages, far fewer paid hours, much safer workplaces) because of rising productivity and capital investment, not unions or government regulation; OSHA and labor law merely codified safety gains that were already happening, and class warfare/nostalgia for the past is misguided.
- Job market for non-college workers — moderate — Young workers without degrees are enjoying unusually strong relative job numbers right now, but a college degree still lowers unemployment in absolute terms; the "value" of many degrees has fallen relative to skilled trades and hospitality work, and people should only pursue college if it actually creates value.
- Gas prices — moderate — Gas hit a Labor Day record ($4.15/gallon, 30% above last year); he blames this on the Strait of Hormuz not actually being reopened despite Trump administration claims, and says Trump has failed to strategically resolve the standoff with Iran.
- AfD's win in Saxony-Anhalt / Germany politics — large — He sees AfD's record win (43.8%) as a predictable backlash against establishment parties' failure on three real issues (mass/Islamic migration, energy policy after shutting down nuclear, and the failing mixed economy), even though AfD wraps these legitimate grievances in illegitimate ethno-nationalism, pro-Russia sympathies, and welfare-statism.
- VW plant sale to Israeli defense firm — small — Praises the sale of a shuttering Volkswagen plant to Israeli private-equity/defense firm (tied to Rafael) to build air-defense systems as a textbook example of capital and labor being productively repurposed when an old industry declines.
- 9/11 Saudi involvement (BBC report) — small — New video evidence ties Saudi citizen Omar al-Bayumi more closely to a 9/11 plotter, reinforcing his long-held view that elements of the Saudi government/intelligence likely helped the hijackers, and that the US has never held Saudi Arabia accountable.
- Civilian casualties in Lebanon/Gaza — moderate — Argues Israel and the US bear no moral responsibility for civilian deaths in strikes against Hezbollah/Hamas because the aggressors (who started the wars and hide among civilians) are at fault; he rejects "proportionality" as an immoral, egalitarian standard and says militaries should use overwhelming force to protect their own troops rather than risk soldiers to spare enemy civilians.
- AI/tech achievements — small — Highlights an AI system assisting real-time brain tumor surgery in the UK and an FDA-authorized autonomous blood-draw robot (Vitestro's device) as examples of technology improving precision and productivity; also cites a Human Progress stat that safe sanitation access has risen from 33% (2000) to 60% globally.
- Q&A (Coke Zero/diet soda, donating to moderate Democrats, safety standards in manufacturing, East German communist nostalgia, becoming a Navy SEAL, Europe de-risking from American tech, Enlightenment thinkers and reason, Texas Senate race) — moderate — Mostly personal/opinion answers; on Texas Senate he calls both Paxton (corrupt, anti-data-center) and Democrat Talarico weak, and says he'd likely sit out or protest-vote to punish Republicans for nominating bad candidates.

## Running threads

- Strait of Hormuz / Iran standoff — continues as unresolved: US quietly spent four months minesweeping but hasn't restored normal shipping traffic, Iran keeps threatening US Navy ships, and he remains skeptical Trump will ever strike Iran decisively.
- Mixed economy causing populism (US and Europe) — reapplied to Germany's AfD surge as the same dynamic driving US populism.
- Israel's wars as self-defense with no moral blame for civilian deaths — recurring framework, applied again here to Lebanon/Gaza strikes.
- Saudi Arabia's culpability for 9/11 — an old suspicion of his, refreshed with new BBC video evidence.

## Moves

- Judges outcomes by long-run material/historical trend lines (wages, safety, leisure, sanitation) rather than isolated bad-news stories.
- Credits productivity/capital/profit incentives, not government regulation or unions, for social progress, treating laws as usually just codifying gains already underway.
- Assigns moral responsibility for war deaths entirely to the aggressor, rejecting proportionality and any standard that asks a defender to risk its own people to spare enemy civilians.
- Looks for the "legitimate grievance" underneath a movement he otherwise condemns (AfD, populism) and separates it from the movement's illegitimate framing (ethno-nationalism, collectivism).
