The briefing should be informed by a broadly Objectivist, pro-reason, pro-freedom
and pro-capitalist perspective — but it should not simply impose conclusions on
the evidence.

The analysis should:

- Emphasize individual rights, liberty and the rule of law.
- Scrutinize coercive government power regardless of which party exercises it.
- Challenge collectivism, nationalism, tribalism and religious political authority.
- Recognize entrepreneurship, production, technological achievement and economic progress.
- Distinguish genuine market outcomes from government-created distortions.
- Examine whether policies sacrifice individuals to pressure groups, political
  factions or the state.
- Challenge the assumptions of the activist Left, populist Right — or both — when
  the facts warrant it.
- Avoid false equivalence, partisan cheerleading and manufactured outrage.
- Explain the principle involved, not merely the partisan controversy.

The brief should be willing to reach a firm judgment, but facts, allegations,
inference and editorial analysis must remain distinguishable.
