# Netanyahu & 10/7; Houtis; Trade War; AI; Parenthood; PISA; Achievements | Yaron Brook Show

## Topics

- Reports that Netanyahu was warned before October 7th (a UAE president's warning reported by Haaretz, and a Shin Bet chief's warning reported by the Jerusalem Post) — about a fifth of the news segment, plus two Q&A answers — The reports are unproven and Haaretz is biased, but they fit his long record: October 7th was a political failure, not an intelligence one, Netanyahu must lose next month's election, and an independent commission should establish the facts.
- US-Canada trade war (Canada's retaliatory tariffs, Trump's threat against Bombardier, China's trade surplus) — about a sixth — Tariffs are taxes on your own citizens that restrict voluntary trade, retaliation just adds to the damage, Canada should have cut its tariffs instead, and a trade surplus does not mean the exporting country is winning.
- OpenAI's chief scientist calling for slowdowns and mandated safety rules, plus an AI-built zero-click worm against WeChat — about a sixth — AI companies should act responsibly and slow down on their own when a tool is dangerous, and government's role is to set liability, not to decide how fast progress goes; handing that power to governments or international bodies is dangerous and would hobble defenders.
- PISA 2025 results — about a tenth — US reading and math are falling and COVID no longer explains it; the cause is a government school monopoly run for the teachers' unions, not a lack of money, so school choice is an acceptable step toward full privatization, and smartphones are a factor but not the explanation.
- Houthi missile and drone attacks on Saudi Arabia — about a tenth — Tit-for-tat exchanges achieve nothing; with US backing, Saudi Arabia should go into Yemen and destroy the Houthis, which would reopen the Red Sea route and set Iran back. He expects the Saudi defense pact with Turkey and Pakistan to prove worthless.
- Positive stories: Uber and Wayve supervised robotaxis in London, ASML's new campus in the Netherlands, a federal loan to restart Iowa's Duane Arnold nuclear plant — about a tenth — All three are progress; London deserves credit for opening to autonomous cars, and nuclear power coming back is good even though he dislikes government loans, with Google's long-term power contract as the right model.
- Virginia mother convicted and put on an abuse registry for letting her five-year-old walk to a neighborhood pond — a short segment — Even if you think it was a bad parenting call, it is not a crime; children should learn independence and risk early, and the conviction ignores Virginia's own childhood-independence law.
- Q&A: Netanyahu versus Trump — short — They differ: Netanyahu is smart and knows what should be done but gives up his principles for power, while Trump is an ignorant narcissist.
- Q&A: other questions (Ayn Rand's cultural influence, whether a principled free-market leader like Milei can win, the Lindsay Clancy mistrial, holding on to anger and hatred, Mississippi's school turnaround, whether AI simulates brains) — short — Politics is downstream from culture and the culture is not ready; Rand helped but gets no sole credit; Clancy was culpable; hatred should guide whom you avoid, not rule your life; Mississippi shows curriculum and teacher reforms work; we know too little about neurons to call AI a brain simulation.

## Running threads

- Netanyahu as a failed prime minister — Adds the new warning reports, repeats that Qatari money and the release of Sinwar happened on his watch, and calls the coming Israeli election the chance to remove him.
- The Iran war and oil chokepoints — The Houthi strikes put the Red Sea and Bab el-Mandeb at risk on top of the already restricted Strait of Hormuz, with Brent crude moving toward $100; he reads the escalation as Iran pushing oil prices up to get Trump out of the region.
- Trump's trade war — Canada's retaliation took effect after talks collapsed in August, and Trump now threatens Bombardier.
- AI regulation and AI safety — Against AI leaders who ask for government restraints; in favor of company responsibility plus clear liability.
- Education decline and school choice — PISA added as fresh evidence; Mississippi raised in Q&A as the counterexample.
- Autonomous cars and nuclear restarts as signs of progress — London joins the list, and data-center demand keeps bringing nuclear back.
- Owed reviews — The Rick and Morty episode, Star Trek and music reviews, and a book on the Ford turnaround that he is close to finishing.

## Moves

- Government's only proper job is protecting citizens' rights, and every failure is judged against that standard.
- A threat that goes ignored is a political failure, not an intelligence failure: ideological enemies cannot be bought off with money.
- If the accused is innocent, an investigation clears him, so refusing one is telling.
- Trade is voluntary and both sides gain; a tariff is a tax on your own people, and "Made in X" is meaningless in a global supply chain.
- Markets belong to the individual buyers and sellers, not to the president or the nation.
- Prefer private responsibility plus legal liability over regulators deciding the pace of technology.
- Government monopolies fail the same way everywhere (why would schools do better than the post office?).
- Take the imperfect step toward freedom, like school choice, as a transition to the principled goal.
- Tit-for-tat, proportional responses never end a conflict; defeat the enemy.
- A bad choice is not automatically a crime, and people become independent by facing risk.
