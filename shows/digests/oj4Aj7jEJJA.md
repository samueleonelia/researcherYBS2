# Answering Capitalism's Critics 1: Benn Jordan's Leveragism | Yaron Brook Show

## Topics

- Launching a new recurring segment answering popular capitalism critics — brief, framing — he wants to systematically rebut viral non-political YouTubers because they reach millions who never hear a defense of capitalism, and their unchallenged "obvious truths" quietly shape the culture.
- Line-by-line rebuttal of Ben Jordan's video on "leveragism" — most of the show — Jordan's claim that things haven't improved and that capitalism has been replaced by a leverage-based system is wrong on both counts: living standards, healthcare, technology and the stock market have improved dramatically wherever the private sector is free, while the real ills he cites (housing, education, cost of living) trace to government, not capitalism, and Jordan never even defines capitalism, treating growth as a precondition of it rather than its product.
- Jordan's claim that private equity/Blackstone manipulate housing to trap families in poverty — he calls this economically incoherent (you can't profit by systematically overpaying and blocking supply) and says Jordan ignores that government-restricted housing supply, not private equity, drives prices up, just as government policy (not "predatory" firms) caused the 2008 crash that let Blackstone provide useful liquidity.
- Jordan's claim that billionaires/capitalists back closed-border, anti-immigration politics out of self-interest, and that Musk/Trump-aligned billionaires are pursuing hidden economic advantage — Brook argues this is economically backwards (labor shortages make businesses poorer, not richer) and that billionaires courting Trump reflects fear of his political/coercive power, not a shared economic interest, showing critics can't separate political power from economic power.
- Happiness/income studies and "shadow work" (IKEA assembly, self-checkout) as evidence capitalism exploits people — he dismisses the happiness-plateau research as methodologically empty and says outsourcing chores to consumers or machines is a benefit of capitalism (lower prices, more choice), not "stolen time."
- Listener question about JD Vance and the appeal of socialism — capitalism, not government-run "stability" schemes, is the actually stable system; bailout-driven stability breeds the resentment that pushes people toward socialism, and it's the well-off/intellectual class, not the working poor, who are drawn to socialism.

## Running threads

- The one-way ratchet of "more statism, less freedom" as the actual explanation for stagnation and rising costs — repeated as his default frame for why lived problems people blame on "capitalism" are really consequences of the mixed economy drifting toward statism.
- The claim that we have never actually had capitalism, only a mixed economy — used again here as the starting corrective whenever a critic blames "capitalism" for something.
- Distinguishing political power (coercion) from economic power (voluntary exchange) as the recurring diagnostic for why critics conflate business influence with government force — he flags this as a pattern he expects to see in every future installment of this series.

## Moves

- Ground the term before arguing: attack critics for using "capitalism" (or "leverage," "shadow work," etc.) without ever defining it.
- Trace any real-world problem to its specific government policy or regulation rather than letting it be blamed on markets in general.
- Reject aggregate/collectivist framing (the "pie," average happiness studies, city-vs-billionaire purchasing power) in favor of individual production and exchange as the real unit of economic analysis.
- Test claims against basic market logic (buy low/sell high, competition constrains prices) to expose "conspiracy theory" economics that don't actually work mechanically.
- Treat objecting to self-interest/profit as smuggled-in altruist moral premises, and defend selfishness/greed as compatible with, not opposed to, mutual benefit.
