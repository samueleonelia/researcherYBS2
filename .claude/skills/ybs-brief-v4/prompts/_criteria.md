## factors

Five things decide which stories reach the brief, and they are not equal on
every story:

1. **Importance and likely consequences.** What changes because of this? A
   ruling that binds other cases outranks a ruling that binds one person.
2. **Evidentiary quality.** Read the note's `WEAK SPOTS`. A big story resting
   on an unsourced claim is a trap, not a lead. This can sink an otherwise
   strong story.
3. **Relevance to what he is arguing about now.** The topics above, but more
   than topic-matching: does the story turn on something he argues about?
4. **Potential for discussion on the show.** Is there an argument here, or only
   an event? A mid-sized story with a real principle in it beats a huge story
   with none. This is why the brief is not a wire feed.
5. **Intellectual or cultural significance.** Some stories matter because of
   what they reveal about how people think, not what they change.

Not partisan balance. Not virality. Not "we should have one from each source."

## labels

| Label | When |
|---|---|
| **Demonstrated** | It works, at scale, now. People are using it and you can say how many. |
| **Emerging** | Real and running, but small, early, or in one place only. |
| **Speculative** | Announced, funded, prototyped, promised, or in trial. Nobody has been helped yet. |
| **Historical** | It happened before, in another time or country, and the parallel is explicit. |
| **Biographical** | One identifiable person did it, and how they did it is the point. |

A proposal, a financing announcement, a prototype or a company's own claim is
`Speculative`, never `Demonstrated`. When two labels both fit, take the weaker.

## tags

Every story that reaches the brief carries exactly one tag:

- **LEAD** — the day's dominant stories, the ones that set the agenda. A story
  several papers ran is usually, but not always, a lead: a story only one paper
  caught can lead if it is big enough.
- **WORTH** — less obvious, especially relevant. This is the closing section of
  the brief, and its job is the story a well-read person would have missed. A
  story that merely did not make the leads is not automatically WORTH; it should
  be something he would be glad to have been shown.
- **BODY** — everything else that is kept. The writer groups these under topic
  headings.
