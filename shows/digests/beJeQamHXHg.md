# Iran; Canada; Netanyahu; UK vs Israel; Mertz vs AfD; DSA; AI; Tucker; Achievement | Yaron Brook Show

## Topics

- Iran/US conflict in the Gulf — large, opening topic — Oil is back above $100/barrel as Iran and the US trade attacks; Iran is firing ballistic missiles at US warships and bases (mostly intercepted) while the US, oddly in his view, is destroying Iranian tankers instead of hitting Iran's missile launchers and stockpiles directly, a strategy he says makes no sense from a president with no strategic sense.
- Trump's trade war with Canada — medium — He mocks the new US move to outright prohibit (not just tariff) Canadian alcohol, dairy, whey protein, and large motorcycles, arguing this looks like an unprecedented and possibly illegal unilateral presidential embargo power, and repeats his standing position that the real fix is zero tariffs and free trade.
- Netanyahu and the October 7 warning stories — large — He doesn't believe Netanyahu literally let the attack happen for political gain (calls that an "ignorant conspiracy theory"), but argues Netanyahu ignored or downplayed real warnings out of arrogant overconfidence in his own read of Hamas, and reiterates that Netanyahu bears responsibility as the 15-year prime minister and must lose the coming election and retire.
- UK vs Israel diplomatic clash — medium — He criticizes Britain's new restrictions on trade linked to Israeli settlements as altruism-driven foreign policy from a country that can't afford it, while also agreeing Israel's settler violence in the West Bank (which his own nephews have encountered in reserve duty) is real and shameful and one more reason Netanyahu's government has to go.
- German election reaction (Merz vs AfD) — medium — He says Chancellor Merz's refusal to name Islamism specifically (talking instead about "ethnic cleansing" and vague remigration) is why AfD is surging, arguing the real fix is surgically deporting Islamist-linked immigrants rather than either AfD's broad-brush anti-foreigner approach or Merz's evasion.
- DSA paid co-chair positions — small — He finds it hypocritical/funny that the Democratic Socialists of America, built on anti-boss anti-employment ideology, just approved ~$95k salaried co-chair positions, seeing it as a tacit admission that organizational leadership is real skilled work, contradicting the "labor creates all value" line.
- AI extinction-risk warnings — medium — He's skeptical of the doom framing from Anthropic's Evan Hubinger and others claiming >10% chance AI kills humanity this decade, noting current AI (Claude, ChatGPT) is already hugely useful, and says industry leaders crying "stop us before we kill you" is what's driving public fear and opposition to data centers.
- US accusations of Chinese AI distillation — small — He notes FBI/NSA/CISA accusations that Chinese firms (DeepSeek, Alibaba, etc.) illicitly distilled US AI models, expects Trump to ban Chinese models citing national security while really protecting US industry, and expects China to retaliate in kind.
- Tucker Carlson interview segment — large/comedic centerpiece — He plays and mocks a Tucker Carlson interview with sculptor Jesse Olafson claiming algebra is pointless/a scam, treating it as proof of Tucker's cognitive decline into "self-caused madness" from a diet of conspiracy theories, not genuine ignorance.
- Human achievement segment — medium — He runs through good-news items: Mexico's large homicide-rate decline, an AI-guided manufacturing breakthrough for a rocket-engine alloy, DeepMind's AlphaGenome atlas of genetic variant effects, and an early-stage gene therapy trial aiming to reverse optic-nerve cell aging.
- London airport IT outage — small — A NATS air-traffic system failure caused mass UK flight cancellations; he expects calls to nationalize the quasi-private NATS monopoly rather than allow competition, which he opposes.
- Super chat Q&A — medium — Covers voting strategy (leans toward not voting given "a pox on both houses," predicts drift toward some future authoritarian consolidation of left/right), Israeli election preference (leaning Bennett, rejecting Netanyahu-aligned or religious-nationalist options), a cost-benefit question about paying $100/year to save 24 hours of driving (he'd pay it, time is worth more), objectivism practice advice (avoid being "secondhanded"), and Hector's decision to fight Achilles (honor-bound, not altruistic).

## Running threads

- Netanyahu must lose the election and retire — continues from prior shows' coverage of pre-October 7 warning stories; today adds the Haaretz/UAE call story, Netanyahu's denial and threatened defamation suit, and his own read that Netanyahu ignored warnings out of arrogance rather than conspiracy.
- Islam/Islamism as the unspoken issue in Western immigration debates — recurring frame, applied this time to Germany's AfD surge and Merz's response.
- Altruism as the hidden driver of foreign policy — recurring argument, applied here to Britain funding the Palestinian Authority despite its own economic crisis.
- Iran war strategy criticized as incoherent — continuing complaint that the US isn't targeting Iran's missile capability directly.

## Moves

- Distinguishes irrationality/incompetence from malice: repeatedly argues actors (Netanyahu, Tucker) are wrong or self-deceived, not conspiring, and rejects conspiracy-theory explanations.
- Applies a consistent "surgical vs broad-brush" standard to immigration policy: name the actual problem (Islamism) precisely rather than either denying it or generalizing to all immigrants/foreigners.
- Uses cost framed in time-value-of-money terms to evaluate personal finance questions from the audience.
- Treats hypocrisy-spotting (DSA salaries, "the enemy of my enemy" reasoning) as a rhetorical device to expose contradictions in collectivist or anti-capitalist claims.
