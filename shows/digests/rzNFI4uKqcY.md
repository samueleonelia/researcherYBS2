# Moscow; Iran; Canada; Data Centers; Bessent; Amargi; Sit Awareness; Achievement | Yaron Brook Show

## Topics

- Trump's Canada tariff war — most time — He calls Trump's claim of a "$60 billion loss" to Canada economically illiterate (trade deficits aren't losses), says the deficit exists only because the US buys Canadian heavy oil, and blames the collapsed trade deal on US cronyism (steel/aluminum lobbies) as much as on Canada's "stupid" retaliatory tariffs.
- CIA director's surprise Moscow trip — large — He reads it as most likely a warning to Putin against testing NATO's resolve (e.g., a probing incursion into a Baltic state), while doubting it will deter Putin any more than a similar 2021 warning did before the Ukraine invasion.
- Bessent's Iran secondary-sanctions threat and US-China tension — large — He argues Bessent undercut his own "financial D-Day" threat by admitting the US won't actually impose it because it would "blow up the global financial system," calling this bluster that will only push China closer to Iran without solving anything.
- Anti-data-center political backlash (Ken Paxton's Texas plan) — moderate — He condemns Paxton's proposal to hold data centers criminally liable for downstream AI misuse as a destructive, trial-lawyer-enriching theory of liability meant to kill the industry, and praises West Virginia's opposite bet of courting data centers (and even coal power) to boost its economy.
- New York Times op-ed proposing ancient "amargi" debt cancellation for the $40 trillion US debt — moderate — He calls the idea economically illiterate and morally monstrous, since defaulting would wipe out pension funds, 401(k)s, insurance companies and banks holding that debt as assets, and argues the real fix is spending cuts/running a surplus, not default or higher taxes (since tax revenue is structurally capped near 18% of GDP regardless of rates).
- Situational Awareness hedge fund blowup (Leopold Aschenbrenner) — moderate — He uses the 24-year-old's 439%-gain-then-67%-loss collapse (forced into a fire sale to Citadel) as a case study that leverage, not the underlying AI investment thesis, is what destroys even correct long-term bets.
- Human achievement roundup (polio eradication in five African countries, IBM quantum cooling milestone, AI-run "self-driving" labs, UK/Mexico growth) — small — He credits vaccination campaigns for polio's elimination in those countries and frames the AI/quantum items as promising but not yet transformative.
- Listener Q&A (Trump as grifter, Dolly Parton tribute, bond vigilantes, Nvidia governance, digital ownership, Canada invasion risk) — small — He describes Trump as having genuinely bad ideas rather than pure grift, praises bond vigilantes as a needed discipline on government, and dismisses fears of a foreign takeover of Canada as absurd.

## Running threads

- Trump's foreign-policy competence — he says he "underestimated how bad Trump would be," now rating foreign policy as the worst-performing area of the administration, citing Canada, Iran, North Korea, China, and Greenland together.
- Iran sanctions/regime survival — continues tracking the sanctions campaign as ineffective bluster while the regime preps a security crackdown (citing a large new security hiring push) against renewed unrest and Iran seeks alternate oil export routes via Iraq/Pakistan to blunt the pressure.
- The $40 trillion federal debt and spending-not-taxes framing — repeats his standing claim that tax revenue is capped near 18% of GDP no matter the rate, so the deficit is purely a spending problem, now driven mostly by entitlement/social-program growth.
- Data centers as a bipartisan political target — continues his recurring complaint that both parties are competing to attack data centers/AI infrastructure, contrasted with states like West Virginia trying to profit from being pro-data-center.
- Trump's "5-year-old economics" line — reiterated and "doubled down on" using the Canada trade-deficit episode as fresh evidence.

## Moves

- Judges policies by an economic-literacy standard: trade deficits, tariffs, and debt default are analyzed strictly through basic accounting/balance-sheet logic to expose politicians' claims as false or self-contradictory.
- Uses historical precedent as a warning: draws on Cold War/Ukraine-2021-style intelligence warnings, Smoot-Hawley, and 1990s Canadian/Swedish austerity to judge whether a current move will work or fail.
- Treats leverage as a near-universal explanation for financial blowups, applying the "10% down payment on a house" mortgage analogy to show how it amplifies both gains and losses.
- Frames government intervention (tariffs, liability threats, subsidies) as driven by cronyism and pressure groups rather than principle, and judges it by whether it expands or restricts individual/economic freedom.
