# 9/11—25 Years Later: Objectivism, Islamic Totalitarianism, and America's Crisis of Ideas

## Topics

- The road to 9/11 and the failure to confront it — largest share of the show — decades of American appeasement (Iran hostage crisis, weak responses to earlier attacks) let Islamic totalitarianism grow unchecked, and this was possible only because altruism and a lack of national self-esteem made Americans unwilling to assert their self-interest and fight back.
- The post-9/11 response and Bush-era whitewashing — large share — Bush and virtually the whole establishment (left academics, and even libertarians like Ron Paul and Cato) evaded that the attackers were driven by Islamic totalitarianism, insisting Islam was peaceful and/or that America was to blame, which he calls a 25-year pattern of gaslighting that destroyed institutional trust.
- The "forward strategy of freedom" and democracy promotion as self-defeating — moderate share — pushing elections in Iraq, Egypt, and Gaza without a prior cultural commitment to individual rights predictably empowered Islamists (Muslim Brotherhood, Hamas), directly causing Hamas's takeover of Gaza and setting up October 7th.
- Reactions to October 7th and campus/left anti-Americanism — moderate share — he reads professors and protesters celebrating October 7th and chanting "Free Palestine" not as concern for Palestinians but as a "hatred of the good for being the good," a nihilistic desire to tear down Western success dressed up in settler-colonial theory.
- The Danish cartoons crisis (2005-06) — moderate share — Western self-censorship and siding with rioters over Jyllands-Posten proved the West would not defend free speech against Islamist intimidation, emboldening the Islamist push into Europe he says continues today.
- Origins of Trump/populism and the Iran war today — moderate share — distrust from 9/11-era lies plus the 2008 financial crisis (elites claiming both total competence and total ignorance) produced populism; the Trump administration's undeclared Iran war and FCC threats against war critics are, in his view, a direct extension of the same contempt for the public and erosion of accountability that started after 9/11.
- Q&A: laws of war, moral relativism, and named individual cases — smaller share — international laws of war and "just war theory" are built to handicap the moral side and empower evil; evil is fundamentally impotent and gains power only through the "sanction of evil" (society's failure to condemn it); American interventionism is not the cause of 9/11 because the ideology's own stated goals (a global caliphate, hatred of a secular successful America) are unconditional, not a response to policy.

## Running threads

- Failure to name/understand the enemy ("Islamic totalitarianism") as the root cause connecting 1979 Iran, 9/11, Iraq/Afghanistan, Gaza, and the current Iran war — reiterated as the organizing thesis of the new ARI book "Failing to Confront Islamic Totalitarianism."
- Altruism as the corrosive force undermining self-esteem and self-defense — applied again to foreign policy, laws of war, and the "package deal" treatment of the poor/weak/suffering.
- The Iran war (ongoing since bombings in 2025 and February 2026) — cited repeatedly as proof the administration doesn't understand Iran's religious/Islamic character and is fighting without explaining itself to Congress or the public.
- ARI's own history of activism after 9/11 (Peikoff's ad, radio/TV blitz, campus outreach, "Winning the Unwinable War" book) — offered as evidence that philosophical framework predicted outcomes correctly over 25 years.

## Moves

- Traces political/cultural outcomes back to which moral philosophy (altruism vs. rational self-interest) a society or leader accepts.
- Uses the "sanction of evil" standard: evil persists only when the good fails to condemn and oppose it, so the fix is always moral clarity and self-assertion, not policy tweaks.
- Draws direct historical parallels (World War II vs. 9/11, Christianity's marginalization vs. Islam's non-marginalization) to argue that identifying an enemy as evil and acting on it is what produces victory.
- Distinguishes an ideology (Islamic totalitarianism) from individuals who identify with the religion, rejecting both whitewashing the ideology and collectivizing blame onto all Muslims.
