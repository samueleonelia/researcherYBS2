# Econ War; N.Korea; Ukraine; Army; DataCenters; Interest; Buy Elections; Rogan; AI | Yaron Brook Show

## Topics

- Trump's "economic D-Day" on Iran — large — He mocks it as empty theater: Trump claims Iran's military is destroyed yet won't open the Strait of Hormuz, and the sanctions push is undermined by the US having tolerated its own allies (UAE, and separately China, Russia, Oman, Qatar) trading with Iran for years; he doubts it will collapse the regime.
- Trump and North Korea — medium — He calls Trump's courting of Kim Jong-un a repeat of a failed first-term strategy that yielded no concessions, made worse now because Trump is negotiating from a position of weakness, and North Korea's missile launches and refusal to meet are evidence Kim is rebuffing him.
- Ukraine air defense collapse — large — He argues Ukraine is now defenseless against Russian ballistic missiles because US Patriot supplies are depleted, and he blames both the exhausted US defense industrial base and Israel's failure (for self-interested reasons) to share its own air-defense systems; he also contrasts the silence over Russian strikes on civilian targets with the outsized global condemnation of Israel.
- US Army abandoning drone modernization — medium — He sees the disbanding of the new drone battalion and reassignment of the 3rd Armored Corps to a Western Hemisphere/drug-war role as evidence Hegseth and his allies are incompetently reverting the military to "basics" while ignoring lessons from Ukraine, and he blames poor political appointments for degrading US military readiness broadly.
- Data centers becoming politically toxic — medium — He argues Republicans and Democrats have converged in hostility to AI data centers (citing Ohio, Texas, Pennsylvania), and faults AI companies and their leaders for failing to make a positive public case for the technology while fueling fear themselves.
- Rising long-term interest rates and US debt — medium — Leaning on economist John Cochrane, he argues the rise in 10/30-year yields reflects a mix of possibly-better AI investment alternatives and a rational "bond vigilante" loss of confidence in unsustainable US/global fiscal policy, not primarily inflation expectations, and dismisses Treasury Secretary Bessent's claim that deficits have peaked as dishonest.
- Money and election outcomes — small — Using several recent primary results, he argues that outspending an opponent does not reliably win elections, contradicting the popular "elections are bought" narrative; correlation between money and winning largely reflects strong candidates attracting money, not money creating wins.
- Joe Rogan segment (reaction to a Rogan podcast clip) — large — He excoriates Rogan and his guests as economically ignorant conspiracy theorists for claiming corporations would violently kill any president who tried to break them up, arguing this reflects an anti-business, anti-elite populism on both left and right that could lead people toward authoritarian solutions.
- California oil/refining self-sabotage — small — He mocks California for shutting down its own refineries and now needing a $6 billion pipeline (enabled by a temporary Jones Act waiver) to import gasoline it could produce itself, calling it a self-inflicted wound with no climate benefit since production just moved to Texas.
- AI competition (US frontier models vs Chinese open-source) — small — He notes Chinese open-source AI models are unprofitable and under investor pressure to add commercial licensing, and flags a Stanford paper suggesting small local models could undercut the value of massive US data-center investment, underscoring how uncertain AI's competitive future is.

## Running threads

- US debt and interest rates — continues a running concern (references "yesterday's" discussion of Treasury bond buybacks); he notes the debt passed $40 trillion and reaffirms his standing prediction of long-term stagnation rather than sudden collapse, while flagging rising risk of something worse if deficits aren't addressed.
- Israel's restraint/precision vs. Russia and Iran's indiscriminate attacks — a continuing contrast he draws to argue Israel is held to a double standard.
- Disintegrationism (DIM) — reiterates that a disintegrated left winning individual elections doesn't refute his thesis that it cannot hold power long-term.
- US military decline under Hegseth — links today's army drone story to a running critique (referenced "the Navy and the stupid catapults" from the prior day) of Trump-administration appointments degrading military readiness.

## Moves

- Judges policy announcements by whether the government follows through, not by the rhetoric ("watch list" standard, expects laws/statements to be ignored by China, Russia, etc.).
- Applies a consistency/double-standard test, comparing how Israel is judged versus Russia and Iran for civilian casualties.
- Attributes economic and political dysfunction to bad ideas and bad personnel (unqualified appointees, altruism, anti-capitalist premises among AI CEOs) rather than to structural inevitability.
- Distinguishes correlation from causation when evaluating data (money and elections).
- Frames cultural/political trends in terms of a "crisis of reason" — people's incapacity or unwillingness for abstract, rational thought.
