## Running storylines, the ones carrying across shows

1. **The Iran war that ended with nothing settled: sanctions bluster and the Strait of Hormuz** — 11 shows. the strikes never found the uranium, Iran rearmed during the pause and is now being handed toll rights over the strait, so sanctions theater like Bessent's 'financial D-Day' is a bluff he says Washington will never call.
2. **Trump's tariff war and the collapse of the Canada deal** — 9 shows. he calls the '$60 billion loss' to Canada five-year-old economics, says Canada should have gone to zero tariffs unilaterally instead of retaliating, and reads the 50% tariffs as cronyism that raises the cost of living Trump promised to cut.
3. **The empty US arsenal and a military being degraded from the top** — 8 shows. Patriot and Tomahawk stocks are drained by Ukraine and Iran while Raytheon and Lockheed block competition, and Hegseth's appointees disband drone units and swap electromagnetic catapults for steam.
4. **The $40 trillion debt, surging long yields, and Bessent's attempts to manage them** — 7 shows. long rates are rising because investors doubt the debt will ever be repaid, buybacks and yield-curve tricks just move money between pockets, and only spending cuts (entitlements) fix it since revenue is capped near 18% of GDP.
5. **Israel squeezed: withdraw-before-disarm in Gaza, and Turkey as the next flashpoint** — 6 shows. he backs Netanyahu's refusal to leave Gaza before Hamas disarms, treats the Syrian air base strike as legitimate preemptive deterrence, and reads the Saudi-Turkey-Pakistan pact as Trump handing the region to Islamist powers.
6. **Ukraine defenseless and Russia testing how far it can go** — 6 shows. Ukraine has no answer left to Russian ballistic missiles, letting it build Storm Shadow parts itself beats shipping finished weapons, and the CIA director's Moscow trip probably warns Putin off a Baltic probe that a 2021 warning failed to prevent.
7. **AI data centers becoming the thing both parties run against** — 5 shows. data centers now poll worse than nuclear plants, Paxton wants criminal liability and Shapiro signed restrictions, and he blames AI companies for fueling the fear instead of making the case.
8. **Democrats' hard-left primary wins: Mamdani, DSA, El-Sayed** — 5 shows. the DSA wing keeps taking nominations because it is the only side with conviction, and its actual programs (city supermarkets, rent freezes) produce the opposite of what cost-of-living voters wanted.
9. **Government taking equity stakes and picking industries** — 4 shows. about 31 equity deals with no congressional vote, plus subsidies and import bans on Chinese robots, are left-style central planning under a Republican label.
10. **Tucker Carlson building a movement** — 3 shows. his ten-point manifesto fuses far-left and MAGA-right around hatred of Israel and of finance, and the Shapiro monologue shows the antisemitic conspiracy underneath; he thinks Tucker running would be more dangerous than Trump.
11. **Trump selling advance access to his own market-moving posts** — 3 shows. the $100,000-a-month Truth Social scheme is insider trading in plain sight and impeachable on its own, the latest entry in a pattern he calls the Picasso of corruption.
12. **His new series answering capitalism's popular critics** — 2 shows. he has started taking viral critics apart line by line, from Benn Jordan's 'leveragism' to Stanford's new encyclopedia entry on capitalism, because millions hear these claims and no rebuttal.

## The argument he repeats most

whatever gets blamed on capitalism, name the specific government policy that actually caused it

Secondary moves:

- economic power is a trade you can walk away from; political power is the gun
- make the critic define the term first, because 'capitalism' and 'leverage' usually dissolve on contact
- do the arithmetic before the morality: show the program cannot be paid for, then say it is also wrong
- apply the same test to both tribes, and check 'pro-market' or 'pro-free-speech' claims against actions rather than labels
- ask who actually pays and who actually benefits, whether it is a tariff, a subsidy or an entitlement
- count the unseen cost, not just the visible gain (Bastiat, applied to equity stakes and industrial policy)
- put the entrepreneur back in the picture: any account of the economy that omits the producer is broken
- hold the policy against its own stated goal and let the contradiction do the work
- trace the idea back to its source, from Marx to the seminar room to the podcast
- let prices carry the information: a planner overriding the market is claiming to know what the market knows
- judge governments by whether they initiate force, not by the good intentions they announce
