# Woke 1.0; Jason Arday; Trump FP; Justice; Water; Heat; Prometheus; Achievements | Yaron Brook Show

## Topics

- Woke 1.0 / Woke 2.0 — largest topic, opening ~45 min — He argues AOC's dismissal of 2020 "woke 1.0" positions as "crazy" is a superficial dodge: the underlying postmodernist, anti-truth, tribal epistemology never went away, it just changed target — on the left it's morphing into class-war Marxism, a wealth tax, price controls, and hatred of Israel/alignment with Hamas, while on the right it has become the same tribal, fact-free dogma around defending Trump and ICE; he calls the whole phenomenon better described as "tribalism" than "woke," and worries the epistemic vice (not caring about truth, only tribal loyalty) now spans the whole political spectrum.
- Jason Arday's fall and suicide — second-largest topic — He lays out the extensive evidence of fabrication (plagiarism, fake credentials, invented charity/sports achievements, invented threats) and argues Arday was a DEI-driven fraud enabled for years because of his "youngest black Cambridge professor" narrative, and that commentators like Sadiq Khan, Zahra Sultana and Ibram X. Kendi calling his exposure a "lynching" are lying; he sides with writers like John McWhorter and Richard Hanania that this exposes how race-based double standards ("reverse racism is still racism") corrupted academic rigor at Cambridge.
- Trump's foreign policy failures — major topic — He calls Trump's foreign policy an unmitigated historic failure: the Iran war ended without strategy, Iran used the ceasefire to rearm (per a WSJ report) while the US did nothing and is now scrambling for missile supplies; Trump undercuts Israel by pressuring it to leave Gaza before Hamas disarms and sends Kushner to personally thank Hamas leaders; Trump fawns over Kim Jong-un and Putin while treating allies like South Korea, Europe, and Israel with hostility or neglect; and he calls the new Saudi-Turkey-Pakistan defense pact a disaster that hands the Middle East to Islamist and nuclear powers at Israel's expense.
- Trump's tariffs and economy — moderate — He restates his long-held view that tariffs are a tax that raise prices and have failed on every stated goal (no manufacturing has returned, China is unaffected, Cuba/Venezuela problems are from embargoes not tariffs), while conceding the broader economy is merely okay, growth is under 2%, and Fed policy plus tariff uncertainty are holding back what should be an AI-driven boom.
- Colorado River water crisis — moderate — He praises a new interstate water-marketplace proposal (desalination, recycling) as a step in the right direction but argues the real solution is full privatization of water rights so prices reflect true scarcity, and blames environmentalists, not energy costs, for blocking desalination in California.
- European heat deaths — brief — He argues the ~16,000 excess deaths in Belgium/France/Germany during a heat wave are self-inflicted by a cultural refusal to embrace air conditioning, and holds this up as an example of resisting human control over environment.
- Prometheus statue at Starbase — brief, positive — He praises the privately-funded, French-built classical bronze Prometheus statue near SpaceX's Starbase as an inspiring revival of heroic figurative art symbolizing technological progress, and mocks the religious right (Blaze Media, Glenn Beck) for objecting to its "pagan" symbolism.
- Justice Department independence — brief — He is skeptical of new AG Todd Blanch's evasive answer on whether the DOJ will remain independent from Trump, noting Blanch was previously Trump's personal criminal defense attorney.
- AI forecasting hurricanes / SpaceX launch record — brief, positive achievement stories — He cites Google DeepMind's cyclone-forecasting AI outperforming existing models, and SpaceX launching two rockets 38 minutes apart, as evidence of technological progress worth celebrating.

## Moves

- He judges ideas by whether they respect facts/evidence ("epistemic" rigor) versus tribal loyalty, applying this standard equally to left and right.
- He repeatedly invokes his own long track record (years of teaching/lecturing on trade) as grounds to dismiss counterarguments rather than re-litigate them each time.
- He applies a consistent moral standard against racial/group-based double standards, arguing that favoritism toward a group ("reverse racism") is still racism and produces bad outcomes.
- He treats privatization and market pricing as the general solution to resource-scarcity problems (applied here to water).
- He frames human progress as humans actively reshaping nature (desalination, air conditioning, dams) against a culture that instead expects people to submit to natural limits.
- He distinguishes political/collectivist violence from individual self-interested crime, arguing culture wrongly excuses the former as noble because of its "greater good" aims.
