## kinds

Five kinds of thing count as a human achievement. Every one of them is
something people did, reported as having been done:

- **Technological innovation** — a thing that works, ships, or is measured
  working.
- **Medical and scientific progress** — a result, an approval, a trial with
  people in it, a discovery.
- **Market adaptation over bureaucracy** — people or firms routing around a
  rule, a shortage or a failing service, and it working.
- **Institutional reform for individual rights and free markets** — a law
  struck, a rule repealed, a court siding with the individual, a monopoly
  opened.
- **Heroic examples** — one identifiable person, what they did, and that
  reason or courage is the point.

## not

Everything else is none of the five, and this is written here once so that the
step that sorts and the step that chooses throw out the same things:

- a plan, a pledge, a funding round, a company's own forecast, a politician's
  promise;
- a poll, or a market rally;
- a setback averted by luck, where nobody did the averting;
- the same bad news it always was, with a hopeful last paragraph.

What those share is that they ask for belief in a future. Something done can be
weighed for how far it has got; something intended cannot be weighed at all.

The exception, and there is only one: a thing announced today that is already
running somewhere — one plant, one clinic, one city — is one of the five,
because the running part is the deed. How much of it is running gets settled
later, once the article has been read in full.
