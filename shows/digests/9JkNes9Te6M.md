# Assassination?; Tucker/Shapiro; Qatar; Rent; Data Centers; Refunds; Achievement | Yaron Brook Show

## Topics

- Tucker Carlson's attack on Ben Shapiro — largest single segment, with two video clips played and rebutted line by line — Tucker's monologue is a calculated antisemitic conspiracy piece (Shapiro as a Jewish/Israeli "employee" secretly directed by unnamed "old people"), and Brook sides fully with Shapiro on substance (pro-capitalism, pro-America, pro-Israel) while also faulting Shapiro for continuing to sanction Tucker, Trump, and Nick Fuentes by engaging or staying silent instead of refusing to platform them.
- The Air Force One decoy/"food cart" story — large opening segment — he finds the episode simultaneously funny and damning: having Trump hide in a catering truck while staff and cabinet officials stayed on the "threatened" 747 makes the administration look weak and reveals warped priorities, and he's skeptical of the claim (sourced to leaked Israeli intelligence) that Iran plotted to shoot down the plane, floating but doubting the theory that Israel exaggerated the threat to push Trump toward war with Iran.
- New York rent and Mamdani's rent-control push — solid segment — rents (average ~$6,655/month in Manhattan) are at record highs specifically because landlords are raising prices ahead of the incoming rent freeze and because existing rent-control/eviction laws keep thousands of units off the market, so Mamdani's policies are producing the opposite of what voters who care about cost of living want.
- Qatar's funding of US universities — short segment — a tiny, wealthy authoritarian state (330,000 citizens) is the single largest foreign donor to American higher education (over $6 billion), which he says should worry people about foreign influence on academic anti-Israel/antisemitic bias far more than concerns about Jewish or Israeli lobbying influence.
- Data centers, land, and water use — solid segment — the panic over data centers "stealing" land and water (invoking Erin Brockovich's reporting) is overblown: total US data-center land use will be roughly Rhode Island-sized, dwarfed by government-driven land waste like corn-ethanol mandates and paid farmland idling, and water/power concerns are being solved by emerging cooling tech and would be solved further by properly pricing water instead of subsidizing it.
- Tariff refunds — short segment — companies (Apple, Nike, FedEx, Amazon, GM, etc.) are receiving and now passing tariff refunds on to customers, which he treats as proof that American companies and consumers, not foreign exporters, actually pay tariffs.
- Human-achievement roundup (eVTOL air ambulance, AED delivery drone, whole-blood delivery drone, Grok 4.6 release) — short segment — praised as genuine technological progress improving emergency medicine and AI competition driving down costs, without extended argument beyond that.
- Truth Social early-access lawsuit — brief mention — calls Trump's practice of selling advance access to his market-moving posts blatantly corrupt ("the Picasso of corruption") even while doubting the lawsuit's constitutional theory will hold up in court.

## Running threads

- Tucker Carlson as recurring villain — continues a running critique of Tucker as anti-Israel, antisemitic, anti-capitalist, and pro-Russia; this episode adds the detailed Shapiro takedown and a callback to Tucker's earlier "debt is slavery" and "demons" material as further evidence of his descent.
- Iran war/munitions shortage — a running thread about the earlier Israel-Iran war being "run badly"; he reiterates his view that the US faces a real ammunition shortfall from that war, evidenced by frantic Pentagon contracting, and downplays the idea that the alleged assassination plot makes a fresh Trump attack on Iran more likely.
- The $20 burrito/cost-of-living debate — ties back into the Tucker/Shapiro fight and recurs in a super-chat exchange; his position: living standards and value for money are actually good, and complaints about cost of living reflect people wanting more (bigger homes, fancier food) rather than being unable to survive.
- Qatar's outsized influence — he has flagged Qatar's gifted plane and dealings with Trump before; this episode adds the university-funding data point as further evidence of concerning foreign leverage.

## Moves

- Judges policy by incentives and market mechanics: shrinking supply (rent control, land-use mandates) raises prices, and properly priced resources (water) drive innovation and lower costs.
- Applies a "sanctioning evil" standard: engaging with, debating, or excusing bad actors (Tucker, Trump, Fuentes) is itself a moral failure that empowers them, regardless of one's private disagreement with them.
- Compares government-caused land/resource waste (ethanol mandates, idle farmland) against private/tech land use (data centers) to show selective public outrage is unprincipled.
- Treats objectivist self-interest as compatible with doing useful but unenjoyable work, framing value by long-term life-enhancement rather than immediate pleasure.
- Reads political rhetoric for embedded conspiracy insinuation (unnamed "employers," "old people") as a rhetorical technique to identify and call out antisemitism without explicit statements.
